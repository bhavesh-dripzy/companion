Khulā — subtract until it breathes

recommended starting point · pure deletion, ships in an hour

Keep exactly three things: the greeting (your personalization is the differentiator — let it own the screen), the mandala (bigger, quieter), and one row of chips — the ribbon, where the thumb already is. The tagline moves into the header next to the wordmark. The subtitle is deleted. The fact demotes to a single whispering line that cross-fades — no pill, no box.

№2

Launcher — show, don't tell

the super-app posture · capabilities become surface, not sentence

The subtitle's job — "here's what I can do" — is real, especially for new users. So do it with tappable tiles instead of a sentence: six capabilities, each with a provider hint where the proof lives ("Swiggy · Zomato"). The chips and ribbon disappear on the empty state — the tiles are the prompts. Greeting compresses to two lines, left-aligned, like a person saying hello rather than a billboard.

№3

Sutra — the input is the product

composer lifted to centre stage · three smart suggestions, max

The boldest read: Companion's product is the conversation, so the composer shouldn't hide at the bottom of a brochure — it floats dead-centre, framed by the mandala, with the mic given equal billing (voice is a first-class input for Bharat). Below it, exactly three suggestions, picked by time and state — evening → dinner & cab home; an open cart always wins a slot. Never five, never two rows.

progressive disclosure · keep everything, show one at a time

If the heritage facts and capability nudges are worth keeping (they are — they're personality), give them one shared slot instead of stacking. A single card rotates gently: heritage fact → live nudge (weather + cab) → cart resume. Same content budget as a tweet. Dots show there's more; nothing else competes with the greeting.

№4·W

Ek Card on the web

same component, desktop canvas · as implemented in ChatArea.tsx

The web UI shares the exact same ChatArea component, so Ek Card carries over for free — the card simply caps at 460px and sits centred under the greeting, which gets even more negative space on a wide canvas. Desktop never had the scroll problem; what it gains is focus: one headline (name in the №1 gradient), one card, one composer, and the mandala finally has room to breathe behind them. The sidebar, ribbon and composer stay exactly where they are today.

Where I'd start

Decision (12 Jun): №4 Ek Card chosen, with №1's gradient treatment on the user's name — implemented in ChatArea.tsx + SmartCard.tsx. See №4·W above for how it renders on the web.

№1 Khulā is the foundation — it's pure deletion, touches one file, and fixes the actual complaint (scroll + text wall) without betting on a new layout. Ship it first.

Then graft №4's smart card into that open space: it rescues the heritage facts from pill-purgatory and gives cart/weather/reminder state a home that pays rent. №2's tiles are the right answer if new-user activation matters more than calm — worth an A/B later. №3 is the strongest product statement but the riskiest layout change; keep it in the drawer until voice usage data argues for it.
