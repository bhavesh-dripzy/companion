sindhu — twenty voyages

Explorations downstream of concept 03. Every mark is anchored in something real from Meluha — the flat-bottomed boats on the Mohenjo-daro tablets, Lothal’s tidal dock, the binary chert weights, the fish glyph that dominates the undeciphered script, the shore-finding birds, the monsoon that powered the Akkad run. Ink geometry + the kiln gradient + one indigo accent, throughout.

★ shortlist

01Naukā — the tablet boat

The flat-hulled river boat exactly as Mohenjo-daro’s clay tablets draw it: cabin amidships, twin steering oars astern. The gradient hull is the cargo of the whole brand.

02Dhruva — steering by the star

Sail inside the horizon ring; one indigo pole star riding above it. Navigation before instruments — hold the unmoving point and go.

03Vāta — the monsoon engine

Meluha’s sailors rode the seasonal winds to Magan and Dilmun and home again. Three gusts of gradient filling an ink sail: trade as captured weather.

★ shortlist

04Mīna — the fish sign

The single most frequent glyph in the Indus script — scholars read it as “min”: fish, and perhaps star. River life and navigation in one undeciphered pun, with its script marks kept.

★ shortlist

05Setu — the Lothal dock

The world’s first tidal dockyard, drawn as it was engineered: a brick basin open to the sea, water held inside, one indigo sail arriving through the gate.

★ shortlist

06Tulā — the binary weights

Meluha’s chert cubes doubled in perfect ratio — 1, 2, 4, 8 — the same stone standard from Harappa to Bahrain. Measurement as trust; the smallest unit glows.

07Mārga — the meander m

The river’s own course writes the initial: one continuous meandering stroke that reads as a lowercase “m”. The letterform candidate of the set.

08Dvīpa — the citadel mound

Every Indus city rose on a made mound between waters — flood-proof by design. The kiln-fired island, the indigo beacon on top, the river on both sides.

09Ūrmi — the anchor ripple

A stone anchor drops; rings spread to the banks. The quietest mark here — one decision, widening influence. Kin to the mihir mandala, but born of water.

10Karṇadhāra — the helmsman

The tablet boats steer with two stern oars — no rudder pin yet invented. The pair of indigo blades is the hand on the course; the hull is everything carried.

11Patha — the star route

Cargo below, constellation above: the arc of stars is the road. The mark for “we navigate by fixed points” — a fine story for an evals or research lab.

12Vāhana — the manifest

Carnelian, copper, cotton — the three cargos Akkad’s ledgers record from Meluha. Three crates on a gradient hull; the middle one is what you make.

13Sāgara — river meets sea

The estuary inside the horizon: the calm upstream line, the working waves below. Where the known river hands over to open water — the moment of shipping.

14Śilā — the pierced anchor

Lothal’s wharf still holds its drilled-stone anchors. Weight with a hole for the rope: stability that is engineered, not assumed.

15Tāraka — the shore-finder

Out of sight of land, Indian sailors loosed a bird and followed it home. Release something from the masthead and let it find the coast — discovery as method.

16Velā — the tide gauge

Lothal’s dock only works if you can read the tide. A graduated post, the gradient waterline crossing it: measurement of a moving thing — very “labs”.

17Ghāṭa — steps to the water

The Great Bath’s staircases descend into water exactly like every ghat built since — a five-thousand-year-old gesture. Descent, immersion, return.

18Kūpa — the ring well

Brick-lined wells ringed every Indus street — private water, public engineering. The radial ticks are the brick courses; the core is the water found.

19Nadī-nagara — city on the river

The grid above, the current below, one block fired warm: civilization as the seam where planning meets flow. Bridges this set back to concept 02, Nagara.

20Saṅgama — the confluence

Sindhu and the lost Sarasvatī meeting and moving on as one stronger stream. The simplest possible “two become more” — a natural mark for a studio of collaborators.

Reading the set: the four starred marks survive the favicon test and carry the clearest stories — 01 Naukā (the literal vessel, warmest), 04 Mīna (the script’s own glyph, most “Meluha” to anyone who knows, beautiful to everyone who doesn’t), 05 Setu (engineering + arrival in one figure), and 06 Tulā (trust and measurement; quietly the best “labs” metaphor of the twenty). 09 Ūrmi and 16 Velā are the strongest dark horses if you want something more abstract. All twenty share the grammar of the original Sindhu mark — ink structure, kiln-gradient life, one indigo decision — so any of them can sit beside mihir labs without a translator.
