nine aspects of the orb

No history pages this time. The source is the product itself: Companion already has a face — the conic dawn-wheel orb users talk to — and a vocabulary: the jali lattice, the gateway arch, the ink pill button, the typing dots, one fluid easing. Each name below is that same orb wearing a different aspect, drawn with nothing but the interface's own primitives. The marks move, because the app does. The logo and the avatar are the same object.

dhyan labs

ध्यान aspect 01 — the still core

Every screen of Companion breathes around the orb. Here the wheel is pushed all the way out to the rim, the mandala rings quiet down, and the centre does nothing at all — a bindu that refuses to spin. Dhyāna is the word India gave the world for exactly this (it walked east and became Zen): attention as an instrument. The part that doesn't move is the part doing the work.

bheem labs

भीम aspect 02 — the button & the mace

The two strongest things in the interface, stacked: the ink pill button users press, and the orb that answers. Together they are the gada — Bhīma's mace, standing upright like a pillar before it is a weapon. Bhīma's real strength was never the swing; it was that he never broke a vow. Strength, in a product, is a send button that has never dropped a promise.

mihir labs

मिहिर aspect 03 — light through the jali

The app lays a faint jali — the carved lattice screen of Indian architecture — over every canvas it draws. Put the morning sun behind it and the body's own ::before becomes the logo: Mihir, the sun, arriving the way light has always arrived in this country — filtered through a screen that turns glare into pattern. The lattice doesn't block the sun; it makes it legible. That's the whole job description.

dhanu labs

धनु aspect 04 — the wheel drawn back

Take the orb's wheel, cut an arc of it, and string it: the bow is the interface's own ring under tension. What it looses is a spark of itself — a small orb already flying, white core intact. Dhanurveda made a science of this; Arjuna made a koan of it ("only the eye"). For a lab the sequence is the brand: hold the gradient still, aim long, release one true thing at a time.

shesh labs

शेष aspect 05 — the loop that holds

Every loading spinner owes Shesha royalties: he held the worlds between universes, the spinner holds yours between sessions. The orb's ring uncoils into a spiral that never closes — śeṣa means remainder, in the Purāṇas and in Aryabhata's arithmetic alike — and at its heart the white core sits perfectly still while the serpent turns around it. The promise underneath every await: nothing you hand us gets dropped.

jatayu labs

जटायु aspect 06 — the last three dots

The most honest element a chat interface ever shipped is the typing indicator: someone is still there. Jatayu was the Ramayana's typing indicator — the old wing who fought a battle he had already lost so his final message would arrive: which way south. The wing lifts off in the dawn ramp, saffron to indigo, and behind it the three dots keep pulsing — a message still composing, already on its way. Witness everything; make the last byte count.

kurma labs

कूर्म aspect 07 — the stack

The product drawn as a totem, bottom-up: the ribbed shell that bears the weight, the spectrum rail it carries, the orb that gets to do the talking. When the churn began and the mountain started to sink, Vishnu became a tortoise and held it up for a thousand years — unseen, load-bearing, version one of infrastructure. India still lays a kūrma-śilā, a tortoise stone, under every temple it raises. Every pixel rests on a layer that never moves.

makar labs

मकर aspect 08 — the gateway

The app's most Indian pixel is a CSS rule: border-top-radius: 9999px — the gateway arch every conversation begins under. The makara has guarded that shape for two thousand years: temple doors, ghat spouts, Ganga's own mount, the arch over every threshold worth crossing. Inside the door, the orb turns like the Sankranti sun crossing into Makara — the turn toward light. The mark is the moment before you type.

ugra labs

उग्र aspect 09 — the fierce setting

Same orb, teeth out. Ugra is the aspect held in reserve — the eighth name of Rudra, the face Vishnu wore exactly once, as Narasimha, for the problem that slipped through every other rule. The wheel grows eight points and spins three times faster than anywhere else in the system — Sudarshana, the discus aspect — but the core stays white and the bindu stays still: calm pilot, terrible wheel. Gentle by default; this setting exists, and that is enough.

System logic: one orb, nine aspects. Every mark above decomposes into primitives the app already ships — the conic dawn-wheel, the white core, the ink pill, the jali, the gateway arch, the typing dots, the spectrum rail — so any of them can collapse back into the plain brand-orb avatar inside the product, and the avatar can bloom into the full mark on the landing page. The logo and the bot are the same object, which no borrowed iconography could have done. Where each one shines: dhyan and shesh are the strongest pure marks (still core, open loop — both read at 16 px); kurma and makar tell the best product stories (the stack, the threshold); ugra is the boldest — a brand with a documented fierce mode; bheem is the wittiest (the button is the mace); mihir is the most beautiful in motion as a loading screen; jatayu belongs to chat itself — the typing dots are already yours. Motion rule inherited from the app: wheels turn at 24s, ugra alone at 7s, dots pulse at 1.4s — all on the one fluid easing.
