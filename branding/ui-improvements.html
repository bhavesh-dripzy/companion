01

Human activity trail

replaces “TOOL EXECUTION FAILED.” badges

Today every agent turn ships developer debris into the chat: provider chips, a red “TOOL EXECUTION FAILED.” badge, a “View trace details” link. Users read that as the app being broken even when the answer is fine. Replace it with a warm, collapsed activity trail — steps, timings, and a one-tap retry — the transparency stays, the dread goes.

Show me today's showtimes for The Odyssey near me.

02

Compare strip

the “zomato vs swiggy” turn becomes a product moment

The backend already fans out to both providers on “compare pizza prices” — but the UI renders two disconnected carousels and makes the user do the math. One comparison strip with the delta called out turns Companion's singular superpower — being above the apps — into something you can see. No other app in India can render this row.

03

Cart dock

commerce state shouldn't scroll away

The cart card renders once, inline, then scrolls out of existence as the conversation continues — users forget what's in it, re-ask, or abandon. A floating dock pill keeps cart state alive across every turn (count · total · provider color), expanding to a snapshot with the checkout CTA. Session-cart data already round-trips on every message; this is pure frontend.

04

Voice stage

give the live conversation a face

Voice mode today is a pulsing red mic button — the conversation itself is invisible until text appears after the turn. A dedicated stage gives it presence: the brand orb breathing through listening / thinking / speaking, live romanized captions as you speak (the transliteration shipped today), and the assistant's status line while tools run, so 10 seconds of Swiggy never feels like dead air.

BEFORE
🎙

transcript appears after the turn ends
AFTER

“main Zomato par kuch order karna chaah raha tha…”
Zomato pe pizza options dekh raha hoon — ek second…
TAP ANYWHERE TO INTERRUPT · ESC TO EXIT
05

Address chip

never deliver to the wrong house again

Food and grocery flows silently resolve to saved address №1. The user only finds out at the cart — or after ordering to the office at 9 PM. A slim, always-visible delivery chip above the composer (auto-picked, GPS-aware per issue #18) makes the resolution visible and switchable in one tap, before any search even runs.
