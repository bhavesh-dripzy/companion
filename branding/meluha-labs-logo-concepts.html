meluha labs

“Meluha” — the name Mesopotamia’s merchants gave the Indus valley. The first planned cities, standardized weights, baked-brick grids, a script still undeciphered — engineering before empire. Same rule as the sibling mark mihir labs: a quiet geometric symbol + a confident lowercase wordmark, on the modern-India palette — with fired terracotta as Meluha’s own signature.

Mudra — the merchant’s seal

Every Meluhan trader pressed a steatite seal into clay — script along the top, figure below — and that stamp was trusted from Lothal to Akkad. The mark keeps the seal’s grammar: four undeciphered script strokes above, and where the unicorn stood, a kiln-fired gradient boss — the maker’s mark for things built to be trusted. The strongest story; instantly a “stamp of origin”.

Nagara — the planned city

Mohenjo-daro was laid out on a grid four thousand years before urban planning had a name — streets at right angles, drains under every lane. Eight ink blocks hold the street plan; the centre block fires in the kiln gradient — the Great Bath at the heart of the citadel. The most “labs” of the three: systems, order, one warm idea in the middle. Unbeatable at 16 px.

Sindhu — the river and the sail

Meluha’s ships sailed the Sindhu out of the world’s first dockyard at Lothal and show up in Akkadian ledgers — “the boats of Meluha docked at the quay.” A horizon circle, the river drawn as a flowing gradient current, and one indigo sail headed somewhere new. The most romantic of the three — voyage, trade, ambition — and the natural hero for a landing page.

Recommendation: 01 Mudra as the primary brand mark — a seal is literally a maker’s signature, the script ticks make it unmistakably Indus, and the kiln-gradient boss keeps it in the same family as mihir labs’ dawn sun. Use 02 Nagara as the app icon / favicon where its grid stays crisp at 16–48 px, and 03 Sindhu as the landing-page hero illustration rather than the logo. Wordmark set in Clash Display SemiBold, all-lowercase “meluha” with “labs” in regular. The Devanagari मेलुहा sits as a secondary line on stationery and the footer — never inside the mark; Meluha’s own script is undeciphered, which is precisely the point: the four strokes stay abstract.
